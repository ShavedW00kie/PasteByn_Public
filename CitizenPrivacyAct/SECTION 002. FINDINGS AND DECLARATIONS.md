SECTION 2. FINDINGS AND DECLARATIONS. The people of Oregon find and declare:

(a) Personal data — including genetic information, biometric identifiers, precise geolocation, financial history, health status, familial and genealogical records, and behavioral and purchasing patterns — is generated by and about individuals as an incident of participating in modern life, and its collection does not thereby transfer indefinite, unaccountable commercial rights to any collecting entity.

(b) The Oregon Consumer Privacy Act established important rights of access, correction, deletion, and opt-out. These rights, standing alone, place the full burden of enforcement on the individual, provide no mechanism for individuals to share in the value their data generates, and do not reliably survive the sale, merger, or bankruptcy of the entities that hold the data.

(c) The dissolution, sale, or bankruptcy of data-holding entities is a recurring commercial event, not a hypothetical one. Any right that terminates automatically whenever its custodian is sold, merges, or fails is not a meaningful right. Obligations created under this Act bind every subsequent holder of the data, without interruption, unless expressly and narrowly excepted by this Act.

(d) Personal data is more accurately understood as licensed, not sold, by the individual to whom it pertains — revocable, non-exclusive, and subject to conditions the individual may alter prospectively — rather than as a commodity transferring full and final title upon first sale.

(e) This Act extends its protections to every natural person residing in, or whose personal data is collected or processed within, the State of Oregon, regardless of citizenship or immigration status.

(f) Nothing in this Act shall be construed to affect the separate licensing of an individual's name, voice, or likeness for a specific creative, journalistic, or commercial production to which the individual has knowingly and specifically agreed.

(g) Nothing in this Act shall be construed to restrict a health care provider's use of an individual's data for that individual's own treatment, payment, or health care operations. This Act does apply to the sale, license, or transfer of de-identified or aggregated data by such a provider for purposes unrelated to the direct care of the individual from whom the data originated.
