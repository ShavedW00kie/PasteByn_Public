SECTION 12. PROCEEDINGS UNDER TITLE 11 OF THE UNITED STATES CODE.

(a) A controller that commences, or against which is commenced, a 
case under Title 11 of the United States Code, and that holds 
personal data of an individual protected under this Act, shall 
provide written notice to the Oregon Department of Justice not 
later than 5 days after commencement of the case.

(b) Upon receiving notice under subsection (a), the Attorney 
General shall determine whether to seek appointment of a consumer 
privacy ombudsman under 11 U.S.C. §332, to appear and object under 
11 U.S.C. §363, or both, and is authorized to take either or both 
actions on behalf of the state and its residents.

(c) Nothing in this Act alters the jurisdiction of a court 
presiding over a case under Title 11 of the United States Code, or 
excuses compliance with federal law. This section exists to ensure 
the state's interests under Sections 4 through 11 are timely and 
consistently presented to that court, consistent with 11 U.S.C. 
§363(b)(1)(B)(ii).

(d) The Legislative Assembly finds that the definition of 
"personally identifiable information" under 11 U.S.C. §101(41A) 
does not clearly extend to genetic, biometric, or precise 
geolocation data, and expresses its support for federal 
legislation, including S. 1916 of the 119th Congress (the "Don't 
Sell My DNA Act"), that would close this gap for genetic 
information specifically. The Legislative Assembly further finds 
that enactment of that federal legislation would not address 
sensitive-tier categories under this Act other than genetic 
information, and that Sections 9 through 11 remain independently 
necessary regardless of its outcome.
